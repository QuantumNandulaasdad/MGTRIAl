LEVEL 3 · FATIGABLE WEAKNESS

Use the muscle repeatedly

With repeated stimulation, the size of the nerve-to-muscle signal naturally falls slightly. Healthy muscle has a safety margin. In MG, fewer working receptors mean the signal can drop below the threshold needed for contraction.

Important: repeated use does not suddenly destroy more receptors. The problem is the already-low safety factor: a normal fall in signal strength during repeated activity is more likely to cause transmission failure.

What the game teaches:

1. Nerves normally release acetylcholine, which binds receptors on muscle.
2. In common AChR-positive MG, autoantibodies reduce receptor function and number and can damage the postsynaptic membrane.
3. This lowers the safety factor of neuromuscular transmission.
4. During repeated activity, transmission can fail, causing fatigable weakness that may improve temporarily with rest.

Educational game only. It is not a diagnostic tool. Symptoms such as persistent drooping eyelids, double vision, swallowing difficulty, or breathing weakness require medical assessment.
